The story in plain English

The business question

A bank that lends against Dubai homes needs to know three things: what a home is worth today, how fast prices are moving, and how much cushion its borrowers have if prices fall. Dubai Land Department publishes every registered sale and rent contract. This project turns those records into the answers a mortgage and real-estate risk team would use.

Finding 1

The simple median hides what prices did

In 2023 the median apartment price per sq m rose +1%, but like-for-like prices rose +17%. That year sales shifted toward cheaper areas, which held the median down while comparable homes got dearer.

So the project's price index compares like with like (a hedonic model of size, bedrooms, location and off-plan status) instead of using a median.

Finding 2

The index tracks DLD's own, and shows turns sooner

Measured the same way, the project's index matches DLD's official index closely: year-on-year correlation of 0.91 to 0.93 for Dubai, apartments and villas.

It reflects turns about 6 months sooner. The likely reason is that DLD's monthly series appears to average the past 12 months (an inference, as DLD publishes no method), while this index uses each month's sales.

Finding 3

2026: villas have turned, apartments are flat

Villa prices peaked in December 2025 and were 11% lower by July 2026. On a year earlier, apartment prices are −0.2% and Dubai overall +1.0%. Treat the latest months as provisional: they are revised as late registrations arrive.

With interest rates held flat, the 12-month outlook for Dubai is +4.6%, but the 80% range runs from −5% to +15%, so a further fall is well within it.

Finding 4

The valuation model beats the comparables a valuer would use

The model was trained on sales up to 2023 and tested on 275,702 sales from 2025 on, which it never saw. Its typical error is 6.8%, and 65% of its values land within 10% of the sale price.

The usual benchmark, the median price per sq m of similar recent sales, scores 9.9% and 50%. For villas the two are close: villa communities repeat a few layouts, so comparables already work well there.

Finding 5

A borrower's cushion is their deposit

Recent buyers bought into a rising market, so almost none owe more than their home is worth today. But at an assumed 80% loan-to-value, a 20% price fall would put 22% of recent ready apartment buyers (20% of villa buyers) in negative equity, and a 30% fall 73% (64%).

This is an illustrative test on assumed loans, not a forecast of losses.

How it's built

One command, make pipeline, rebuilds everything from the raw files: about 5 GB of CSVs into a PostgreSQL database, then models, then the views Power BI reads.

Data quality and testing

  • Every push is tested in GitHub Actions: the full pipeline runs on small real extracts of the DLD files, with dbt tests on every model.
  • Every report number is reconciled. Each headline figure is computed twice, once from the cleaned data and once from the views Power BI reads, and the build fails if they differ.
  • The totals match DLD's. Yearly registered values agree with DLD's published figures within about 1%.
  • No peeking at the future. A test rewrites every later sale and checks the valuation model's inputs do not change. Models are tested on later years only, never on a random split.
  • Problems are flagged, not deleted. Rows that fail a quality rule keep a flag and a logged reason, so every number can be traced.

Limitations

  • The UAE interbank rate (EIBOR) is not loaded yet, so the US Fed Funds rate stands in for it (the dirham is pegged to the dollar).
  • The forecast's rate effect comes out positive and not statistically significant: the 2022 rate rises coincided with the boom.
  • The stress test is illustrative: loans are assumed from the central bank's caps and held at their starting amount.
  • The latest index months are provisional and change as late registrations arrive.
  • DLD doesn't say whether a villa's area is the plot or the house, so villa and apartment prices per sq m are not compared.
  • Building age and developer are not in the data used, and all prices are in nominal dirhams.
  • 25 Sep 2026 is the data cut-off, so 2026 is a part year.
